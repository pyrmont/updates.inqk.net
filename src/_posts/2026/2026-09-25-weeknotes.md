- It was Silver Week ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Week)): a five-day weekend where Monday was Respect for the Aged Day, Wednesday was Autumnal Equinox Day and so under Japanese law, Tuesday was an ‘extra’ holiday.

- I was thinking that I didn’t remember a five-day weekend last year and it turns out it’s only in years where you have Respect for the Aged Day be Monday and Autumnal Equinox Day be the Wednesday. This triggers the application of the rule under Section 3(3) of the Act on National Holidays (国民の祝日に関する法律) (a copy of which is [available in English](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/4846/en)). The rule turns an ordinary weekday into a public holiday if it’s surrounded by two public holidays. This doesn’t happen very often: the next time will be 2032.

- Does all of that ring a bell? Almost certainly not for you but I did wonder if I’d written about this before and it turns out yes. In [this weeknote](https://updates.inqk.net/post/1664225220.html) in _2022_, I wrote:

  > I was going to call the week ‘Silver Week’ but after a quick glance at [Wikipedia’s entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Week)) I realised that wouldn’t be correct. Silver Week refers to a rare occurrence where a week in September will have three consecutive holidays. Since the holidays here were on Monday and Friday that doesn’t technically qualify. Instead we’ll have to wait til 2026.

We didn’t have time to jump on the E7 and so waited for the next train. To my immense surprise (and I’m a little ashamed to admit delight), the train that pulls up next is the E2 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E2_Series_Shinkansen)). I could hardly believe it. The E2 is the last active service Tohoku Shinkansen model I’ve ridden and, prior to Tuesday, I’d honestly given up much hope that was ever going to happen. It runs a handful of times a day (maybe it’s only once at this point?) and there’s apparently no easy way to determine when this will be. It was great to be able to tick it off the list.
