- [Crossmate](https://apps.inqk.net/crossmate) is now at v1.2.0. The biggest new feature is the chronicle, a compressed record of an entire game (including all player moves). The idea is to allow the original database records to be destroyed after the game with each player retaining their own (significantly smaller) independent copy. That way, if another player deletes the records of a game, it doesn’t impact any other players. I had something like a chronicle (called an archive) but it wasn’t trying to completely replace the game record. I decided to try a different approach after noticing that Crossmate is already using 60MB of my iCloud Storage. Honestly, I’m not sure how that’s possible given how the only data being stored is textual. My theory is that Apple creates additional metadata when you update records in iCloud’s database and because of how many updates can occur in a single game in Crossmate, that can build up to be quite significant after a month or so. By replacing all of that with a single record, I’m hoping the impact on storage will be negligible.

- Nikhil Suresh wrote a [blog post about the current ‘A.I.’ moment](https://ludic.mataroa.blog/blog/ai-mania-is-eviscerating-global-decision-making/) that I thought was very good. Suresh argues many executives realise the reality of LLM-based systems does not match the hype but that these executives are incentivised not to say this publicly because it risks embarrassing their suppliers/customers. I think I’m more optimistic about the potential of LLM-based systems than Suresh (I prefer to call the technology ‘LLM-based systems’ but we’re talking about the same thing). My own opinion is that LLMs in the 2020s are similar to personal computers in the late 1970s and early 1980s. The difference, at least as I understand it, is that the enthusiasm towards computers was somewhat tempered. There’s very little temperance around this technology at the moment.
