- On Thursday, I was going to make a snarky joke on Bluesky about nobody caring about Meta's [Muse](https://muse.ai) a couple of weeks post-launch, but I wasn't sure that was true and so called [Brandon](https://sangsara.net) instead. We had a 30-minute chat while I walked from my office to Yurakucho Station discussing Muse and this generation of cloud-based agents. I expressed my scepticism about the entire category on the basis that I don’t believe ordinary people want to enhance the productivity of their non-work life. Brandon reported on his experience using this kind of agent as well as relaying how Meta has been rolling Muse out in Singapore. After the discussion, I’m now at the point where I think cloud-based agents are at the desktop PC phase of the consumer adoption curve. That is, this is like the 90s where many people had computers in their homes but few people used them intensively and it took until the smartphone a decade or so later before intensive, daily computer use had become completely normalised.

- On Friday, I downloaded Muse and spent about 10-15 minutes creating a ‘service’ that will monitor the weekly screening schedule at the two cinema locations closest to our house and let me know every Thursday whether there are any late night sessions for English-language films on the upcoming Friday, Saturday or Sunday. This is something that I occasionally do but it’s always a bit of a pain. It’s also the kind of thing that’s fiddly enough that writing a traditional program isn’t something I’ve ever been minded to do. I’ll try to remember to report back in a month or two about how useful I’ve found this.

- Speaking of traditional programs, I made another thing! [Augite](https://github.com/pyrmont/augite) is a static site generator for Git repositories. That is, you can point it at a Git repository and it will generate a website for it that allows a user to explore the repository from the comfort of their web browser. It’s similar to [stagit](https://codemadness.org/git/stagit/) but with (1) a responsive stylesheet for a more pleasant mobile experience, (2) a dash of JavaScript for improved directory traversal and (3) syntax highlighting! It’s now what I use for [code.inqk.net](https://code.inqk.net) and I think it’s pretty spiffy.
