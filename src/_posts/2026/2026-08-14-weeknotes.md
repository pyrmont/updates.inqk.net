- Speaking of the news, I appreciated the [most recent episode](https://geopolitical-cousins.captivate.fm/episode/the-king-of-israel) of _Geopolitical Cousins_ (the podcast by two geopolitical analysts, Jacob Shapiro and Marko Papic). I mentioned this podcast [last](https://updates.inqk.net/post/1750773600.html) [year](https://updates.inqk.net/post/1752588240.html) but to recap: it’s a podcast where geopolitical analysts Jacob Shapiro and Marko Papic discuss recent geopolitical events from the perspective of analysts who write advisory notes to investment clients. They’re clear it’s not investment advice (and I don’t use it for that) but I appreciate that it gives their discussions a different flavour to what you tend to get from journalists or academics. They’re also on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@GeopoliticalCousins).

- I mentioned last week that I had used LLM-based coding agents to work on an incomplete Janet project and I wondered whether I would use it to work on [Persimmon](https://github.com/pyrmont/persimmon). I did indeed do this and then I moved on to _finally_ making my own Janet dialect. [Back in 2020](https://updates.inqk.net/post/1604904720.html) (!), in the same weeknote where I noted that Joe Biden had won the presidential election (!!), I mentioned that I had written a parser I called Claret that parsed a more Clojure-like syntax into Janet data structures. Well, once I had the persistent immutable data structures available with Persimmon, I decided to make a standalone program that uses Janet’s VM but swaps out Janet’s immutable data structures for Persimmon’s. I’m not sure whether it’s a good idea or not but this more ambitious [Claret](https://github.com/pyrmont/claret) is now up on GitHub.
